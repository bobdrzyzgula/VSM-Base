# MCU controller, DC-motor variant: draft schematic

*Drafted by Claude at Bob Drzyzgula's direction, October 2026. Not built or
tested.*

**A concept, not a design.** Drawn but not built, simulated or tested;
part values are first estimates; the core board is drawn by GPIO number;
the firmware does not exist. The motor-control paper (§11, "A DC-motor
variant") describes it, and *Inside the Motor* (§9) gives the reasons for
it.

`mcu_controller_dc.kicad_sch` (KiCad 7) is the mains controller of
`hardware/mcu-controller/` with its power stage replaced, to drive a 24 V
brushed permanent-magnet motor of the "775" size in place of the machine's
universal motor. `mcu_controller_dc.pdf` is the same sheet printed.

- **Unchanged:** the control side. That is the RP2350B core board, the
  knee, pedal, pressure-pedal and speed-limit inputs, the heel switch, the
  needle and single-stitch switches, the hand-wheel index sensor and the
  optional encoder, all with the same parts, values and GPIOs.
- **Gone:** everything that touched the mains. That is the IEC inlet, MOV,
  triac, opto-triac, zero-cross optocoupler, motor-enable relay, isolated
  supply and current transformer.
- **In their place:** a 24 V input from a certified adapter, a 5 V buck
  module, an off-the-shelf H-bridge, and sensing for the motor current, the
  bus voltage and the motor's terminals.

The file is generated by `tools/make_dc_schematic.py`, which shares its
symbol code and control-side tables with `tools/make_mcu_schematic.py`. It
prints the netlist it intends (`netlist.txt`), and
`tools/check_mcu_netlist.py` confirms that KiCad's own netlist export
matches it. `tools/export_schematic.sh` makes the paper's figure.

## What is on the sheet

**24 V in.** A two-pin input (J1) from a 24 V adapter with a safety
listing, a 5 A blade fuse (F1), an SMBJ30A TVS diode (D1) for short spikes,
and bulk capacitance: 1000 µF, 50 V, low-ESR (C1), mounted at the driver's
+/− pads, plus 1 µF ceramic (C2).

- **The adapter.** A 24 V, 4 A (96 W) adapter listed as Class 2 or LPS
  (limited power source) is power-limited by its own design, a second line
  of defense behind F1. A 775 at its rated point draws about 3.5 A. If a
  heavier seam needs more, a 5 A (120 W) adapter is the step up, and the
  fuse and wiring then carry the safety on their own.
- **No isolation needed.** Every wire on the sheet is at 24 V or less, so
  the logic shares the power ground, and the adapter does all the
  isolation from the mains. The return currents still need care: keep the
  motor's current out of the logic ground by running both to one star point
  at the driver's GND.

**5 V.** PS1 is a 24 V to 5 V, 1 A buck module (Traco TSR 1-2450, or any
7805-pinout switcher rated to 36 V in), with 10 µF in and out. It feeds the
core board's VSYS through D2, as in the mains version, so USB can stay
plugged in.

**The H-bridge.** A2 is Pololu's G2 High-Power Motor Driver 24v13, a small
module with discrete MOSFETs and a gate driver. It is rated 6.5–40 V and
13 A continuous, with reverse-voltage protection, a current-sense output
and current limiting
([Pololu](https://www.pololu.com/product/2992)).

| PWM | DIR | Outputs | Operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| H | H | A high, B low | forward |
| H | L | A low, B high | reverse |
| L | X | both low | brake |

- **Pulled to a safe state.** The board pulls PWM low and SLP high
  itself, so a resetting or unprogrammed MCU leaves the motor braked.
- **The current limit.** The default limit is about 30 A, more than the
  775's 24 A stall current (24 V across about 1 Ω). A resistor from VREF to
  GND lowers it: R4, nominally 47 kΩ, to be chosen on the bench for about
  8–10 A with the motor stalled. Pololu gives 100 kΩ as about 16 A and
  calls the setting imprecise.
- **Current sense.** CS gives about 40 mV/A plus a 50 mV offset, and only
  while the bridge is driving: it reads low while braking. It goes to ADC4
  through 1 kΩ (R3), with no capacitor, as Pololu asks.
- **Fault.** FLT is open-drain and uses the MCU's internal pull-up.

**Brake chopper (optional, not fitted).** Q1, a logic-level MOSFET, can
switch a 10 Ω, 50 W resistor (R9) across the bus from GP1. It is there for
the case where braking pumps the bus voltage up (see the cautions).

**Sensing.**

- **The bus:** VBUS_S reads the driver's VM pin (after its reverse-voltage
  protection), divided by 11 (100 k / 10 k), into ADC7.
- **The motor's terminals:** VA and VB read OUTA and OUTB, each divided by
  11, into ADC5 and ADC6. Their difference is the voltage across the
  motor. With the driver asleep (SLP low), so its outputs float, that
  difference is the back-EMF, and so the motor's speed.

**At the motor.** C19 (100 nF) goes across the motor's terminals, and two
47 nF capacitors go from each terminal to the motor's case, mounted at the
motor, with the leads twisted. These are the usual suppression for brush
noise.

## Pin use (RP2350B)

Changed from the mains version:

| Signal | GPIO | Use |
|---|---|---|
| PWM | GP4 | bridge PWM, 20 kHz (PWM slice 2, channel A) |
| DIR | GP5 | direction (was RELAY) |
| SLP | GP6 | driver sleep, low = asleep (was ZC) |
| FLT | GP13 | driver fault, active low (internal pull-up) |
| BRAKE | GP1 | brake chopper (optional) |
| ISENSE | GP44 / ADC4 | motor current, from CS |
| VA, VB | GP45, GP46 / ADC5, ADC6 | motor terminal voltages |
| VBUS_S | GP47 / ADC7 | bus voltage |

Unchanged: ENC_A, ENC_B (GP2, GP3), HALL (GP7), STITCH (GP10), HEEL (GP11),
NEEDLE (GP12), and KNEE, PEDAL, FSR, LIMIT (ADC0–3). All eight analog
inputs are now in use.

## Firmware outline

Plain C on the Pico SDK, as for the mains version.

- **PWM.** 20 kHz on GP4, sign-magnitude: DIR fixed forward, the duty cycle
  setting the average voltage, braking in the off-time.
- **A current loop,** at a few kilohertz. The ADC samples CS in the middle
  of the on-time, triggered from the PWM counter. At very small duty
  cycles the on-time is too short to sample, and the loop relies on its
  last estimate.
- **A speed loop around it,** from the encoder or the index. The pedal,
  knee, limit knob and switches set the speed command, as before.
- **Stops become exact.** The mains version cuts firing early and learns
  how far the machine coasts. Here the controller brakes the motor
  electrically, then holds the needle at the taught angle with current: a
  position loop around the speed loop, using the encoder. The heel tap and
  the taught stops are unchanged.
- **Speed without a hand-wheel sensor.** A PM motor's back-EMF is its
  speed, $e = K_e\omega$. With the driver asleep for a moment, VA − VB reads
  the back-EMF directly. While driving, the speed can be estimated as
  $(D\,V_{bus} - I R)/K_e$. Either gives load compensation for the
  pedal-first, feedback-free mode, something the universal motor can't
  offer.
- **Safety.** Nothing drives until the speed command is zero after power-up.
  The hardware watchdog resets the MCU, and a reset leaves the bridge
  braked. A stall watchdog drops to zero if the encoder stops while the
  current is high.

## Cautions

- **Regeneration.** Slow the machine by braking (PWM low), which
  dissipates its energy in the motor's own resistance. Never reverse the
  motor at speed, and don't let the speed loop drive negative current. Both
  push energy back into the bus, and the adapter can't take it back. The
  machine and motor store a good deal of energy: about 25 J for the
  inertia used in *Inside the Motor*'s simulations at 7,000 rpm. Put into
  1000 µF, that would raise the bus far past the driver's 40 V. The
  firmware should watch VBUS_S, and if the bus rises past about 28 V, stop
  driving and fire the chopper (if fitted). D1 catches only short spikes.
- **Heat.** The G2 has no over-temperature shutdown. Give it airflow, and
  keep the current limit at what the motor needs.
- **The speed ratio.** A 775 turns about 7,000–8,000 rpm at 24 V, several
  times the hand wheel's top speed, so it drives the machine through a
  reducing pulley. The ratio is a mechanical matter for the motor bracket.
- **Values to check on the bench:** R4 (the current limit); whether C1 is
  enough at the PWM frequency (Pololu asks for at least a few hundred
  µF); and the dividers' resistance (about 9 kΩ seen from the ADC), which
  is acceptable for slow readings.
